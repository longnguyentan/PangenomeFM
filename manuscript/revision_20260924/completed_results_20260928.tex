\subsubsection{A three-class extension tests whether the signal persists beyond INS/DEL}
\label{sec:sv-type-extension}

We extended known-event classification to insertions (INS), deletions (DEL) and inversions (INV), representing each event by its first affected base rather than the two-endpoint representation of the original INS/DEL task. The analysis retained all 174,267 primary-chromosome events at their observed class frequencies, including 298 inversions. Frozen $T$ increased macro one-versus-rest average precision (AP) from 0.4070 to 0.4740 strict and 0.4586 one-hop. This metric gives equal weight to the AP for each class against the other two classes. After measured event log length $L$ and 14 graph-statistic features $H$ were included, gains remained 0.0477 (\CI 0.0399--0.0553) and 0.0150 (0.0077--0.0214), respectively (\cref{tab:sv-natural}).

The macro improvement did not establish an inversion-specific benefit: both length- and $H$-adjusted INV intervals included zero. Nor did it isolate pretraining, because the extension has no matched random-encoder arm. A sensitivity analysis matching chromosome and length-bin distributions, with 223 events per class, yielded inconclusive gains from adding $T$ after $H$ (\cref{tab:s-sv-matched}). The natural-frequency macro contrasts had exact fold sign-flip $p=0.0625$ and Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) adjusted $q=0.0741$ within the metric; their positive bootstrap intervals for individual comparisons do not establish a benefit after correction for multiple comparisons. All estimates concern the ascertained callset rather than population SV frequencies.
