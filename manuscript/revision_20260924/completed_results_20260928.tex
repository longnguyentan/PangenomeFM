\subsubsection{A three-class extension tests whether the signal persists beyond INS/DEL}
\label{sec:sv-type-extension}

We extended known-event classification to INS, DEL and INV using a common first-affected-base anchor, distinct from the historical two-endpoint INS/DEL representation. The natural-frequency analysis retained all 174,267 primary-chromosome events, including 298 inversions. Frozen $T$ increased macro one-versus-rest AP from 0.4070 to 0.4740 strict and 0.4586 one-hop. After measured event log length $L$ and 14 graph-statistic features $H$, gains remained 0.0477 (\CI 0.0399--0.0553) and 0.0150 (0.0077--0.0214), respectively (\cref{tab:sv-natural}).

This macro result does not establish an inversion-specific benefit: both length- and $H$-adjusted INV intervals included zero. Nor does it isolate pretraining, because the extension has no matched random-encoder arm. A common-support sensitivity with 223 events per class yielded inconclusive $T$-after-$H$ gains (\cref{tab:s-sv-matched}). The natural-frequency macro contrasts had exact fold sign-flip $p=0.0625$ and within-metric BH $q=0.0741$; their positive pointwise bootstrap intervals are not multiplicity-controlled confirmation. All estimates concern an ascertained callset rather than population SV frequencies.
