\begin{abstract}
Human pangenome graphs encode alternative sequence arrangements that are absent from a single linear reference. We introduce PangenomeFM, a topology-native self-supervised framework that couples coordinate attention and graph message passing to learn reusable segment representations. Masked-connection pretraining uses no downstream biological labels; frozen encoders are evaluated with chromosome-held-out probes and matched feature controls. Adding graph representations to coordinates and frozen nucleotide embeddings improves regulatory-element AUPRC by 0.0034--0.0040 and known insertion/deletion classification by 0.0342--0.0401. A three-class extension retains macro gains after event-length and graph-statistic controls, but inversion-specific effects remain uncertain. EN-TEx and external tasks delimit this reuse through modest, null and negative findings. Degree-shortcut and random-encoder audits further distinguish available graph information from benefits of learned weights. The framework establishes task-dependent complementarity between population graph structure and sequence, while identifying the controls needed to evaluate broader pangenome representation learning.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} human pangenome; graph representation learning; self-supervision; structural variation; regulatory genomics

\label{sec:intro}

Human genome representations determine which sequence relationships an analysis can express. Linear references, including GRCh38 and the telomere-to-telomere CHM13 assembly, provide indispensable coordinate systems \citep{schneider2017grch38,nurk2022complete}. A single path, however, cannot directly encode the alternative arrangements carried by different chromosomes. Human pangenome graphs represent shared segments together with connections through alternative sequences. Haplotype-resolved assemblies from HPRC and HGSVC make this population representation increasingly practical \citep{hprc2023draft,hgsvc2021,hgsvc32025}. Shared and alternative segments can then be considered within one data structure, while genomic coordinates still link those segments to existing annotations. Learning from this structure requires a model to handle orientation and connectivity as well as linear position.

This choice of representation matters beyond variant cataloguing. Assembly-based pangenomes improve access to structurally diverse loci and support variant discovery and genotyping \citep{hprc2023draft,hgsvc32025}. Genome choice also affects functional-genomics measurements and their interpretation \citep{maciasvelasco2026benchmarking}. These observations motivate representations that connect molecular sequence content to the population structures in which that sequence occurs. They do not imply that graph features will improve every functional prediction task. A useful representation must demonstrate that its additional information survives comparison with the sequence and coordinate features already available to an analyst. Regulatory and structural-variant tasks provide contrasting tests because their labels capture different aspects of genome organization.

Genomic foundation models offer a complementary approach: self-supervised learning turns large sequence collections into reusable features. DNABERT and Nucleotide Transformer learn nucleotide context and support regulatory-element and variant-related prediction \citep{ji2021dnabert,dalla2025nucleotide}. Their input sequences can contain substantial population diversity, but individual sequence embeddings do not explicitly encode the graph relationships among alternative segments. Whether those relationships contribute predictive information after sequence is accounted for remains an empirical question. Freezing both representations during downstream evaluation makes this question more explicit: biological supervision trains the same probe, while each representation contributes information acquired before that task. Gains can then be attributed to the available feature sets, subject to matched-example and split controls.

A pangenome graph exposes branching, reconvergence and connectivity between shared and alternative segments. Two segments with similar nucleotide composition may occupy different structural contexts; conversely, related graph neighborhoods may contain divergent sequences. These relations motivate learning directly from connectivity while retaining genomic position. Genomic proximity and graph adjacency also define different neighborhoods, providing a reason to model both rather than assuming one can replace the other. The aggregate SV-resolution graph used here does not preserve donor-specific paths, so neither haplotype frequency nor personal allelic effects can be assumed to be encoded by its node representations.

Existing approaches use pangenomes in different ways (\cref{fig:paradigms}). DeepGene converts graph-derived information into sequence representations for foundation-model learning \citep{zhang2025deepgene}; PangenomeX learns a phylogeny-guided network of copy-number relationships for a supervised objective \citep{xue2025pangenomex}. These designs motivate a distinct evaluation of topology-native self-supervision: can representations learned from native connectivity, without biological labels, be frozen and reused across tasks? Answering this requires both biological transfer and controls for information already captured by coordinates, sequence and inexpensive graph summaries. A second distinction is equally important: an untrained graph encoder can already transform those inputs. Demonstrating graph utility therefore does not, by itself, demonstrate that self-supervision has learned a better representation.

Here we introduce \method as a method for this controlled reuse. Oriented-segment inputs preserve native connections; a layerwise gate couples coordinate attention and graph message passing; masked-connection pretraining precedes frozen biological evaluation. The contribution is this pangenome representation and transfer framework, rather than a new attention operator or loss. We test regulatory annotations, known SV types and external outcomes under chromosome holdout. Paired feature comparisons quantify complementarity, while random encoders and shortcut audits test its origin. The resulting evidence supports task-dependent graph information and identifies limits on claims about learned-weight superiority.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{PangenomeFM couples genomic position and connectivity for frozen reuse}
\label{sec:method-result}

The method separates representation learning from biological supervision (\cref{fig:method}). Each oriented graph segment receives seven numeric features. Coordinate attention organizes context by genomic position, while graph attention follows visible connections; both branches update a shared hidden state through a learned gate, residual connection and normalization. Original v1 pretraining predicts masked connections with an ordered pair scorer. Afterward, the 48-dimensional segment vectors $T$ are fixed, and only downstream probes receive biological labels. $T$ therefore contains coordinate and structural information rather than adjacency alone.

Five rotating chromosome folds and three archived executions per fold evaluate strict regional and expanded one-hop contexts. Coordinates $C$, k-mer composition $K$ and independently pretrained sequence vectors $S$ supply matched baselines. The primary contrast, $\Delta_{T\mid C,S}$, measures the AUPRC change when $T$ is added to $C+S$. Handcrafted graph features $H$ and random encoders $R$ ask the separate question of whether learned weights contribute beyond cheap structural representations. These comparisons use identical examples within each contrast; neither pretrained encoder is fine-tuned with biological labels.

\subsubsection{Sequence coverage is complete, but long-segment context is restricted}
\label{sec:sequence-coverage-result}

The pinned Nucleotide Transformer pipeline produced 512-dimensional vectors for all 303,425 segments required by the original cCRE and INS/DEL probes. No example was lost to missing sequence features. However, 26.3\% of required segments exceeded the input limit and used predefined sampling from their two ends. Complete segment coverage is therefore not complete nucleotide coverage. Comparisons involving $S$ concern this frozen representation, not the maximum information recoverable by every sequence model (\hyperref[sec:s-coverage]{Supplementary Information, sequence coverage}).

\subsection{Regulatory transfer is complementary to sequence and task dependent}
\label{sec:ccre-result}

Candidate cis-regulatory element prediction tests biological reuse without regulatory-label adaptation of the encoder. Coordinates plus sequence reached AUPRC 0.9185; adding $T$ reached 0.9225 under strict context and 0.9219 under one-hop context. Paired gains were 0.0040 (\CI 0.0029--0.0048) and 0.0034 (0.0017--0.0048), respectively (\cref{fig:ccre-result,tab:downstream}). Sequence remained the larger independent information source, adding 0.0248/0.0261 beyond $C+T$ (\cref{tab:s-factorial}). These feature gains alone do not establish a benefit from pretraining over random graph encoders.

Subsetting the same binary probe's predictions gave strict gains of 0.0058 for promoter-like elements, 0.0209 for proximal enhancers, 0.0054 for distal enhancers and 0.0349 for CTCF-only elements versus common background. These are subtype evaluations, not separately trained classifiers. Native low-, medium- and high-complexity cCRE gains were 0.0018, 0.0014 and 0.0076; that pattern is specific to this annotation task.

EN-TEx provided a broader test of regulatory activity \citep{rozowsky2023entex}. Its 450 completed fold/seed/context runs cover primary tasks, exposure-matched and assay-specific sensitivities, five tissues and additional assays; they are not independent biological replications. AS-prone cCRE prediction was inconclusive, including a strict gain of 0.00021 ($-0.00032$ to 0.00075). Active-versus-repressed distal enhancer tissue-macro gains were also inconclusive: 0.00089 ($-0.00039$ to 0.00233) strict and 0.00080 ($-0.00153$ to 0.00247) one-hop. Exposure matching changes ascertainment and prevalence, so absolute AP across matched and primary populations does not measure model improvement.

CTCF allele-specific SNV gains were 0.00147 (0.00010--0.00355) strict and 0.00225 (0.00096--0.00348) one-hop. H3K27ac was inconclusive under strict context, 0.00069 ($-0.00057$ to 0.00192), and positive under one-hop context, 0.00334 (0.00012--0.00796). RNA ASE, ATAC and H3K27me3 intervals included zero in both contexts. H3K4me3 gains had positive pointwise intervals, with estimates 0.00442/0.00900. All endpoints remain reported (\cref{tab:s-entex}); low absolute AP and multiple comparisons limit broader claims.

\subsection{Graph representations inform known SV types beyond sequence}
\label{sec:sv-result}

The historical SV task predicts insertion versus deletion among known variant records. Adding $T$ to $C+S$ increased AUPRC from 0.8721 to 0.9063 strict and 0.9122 one-hop, giving paired gains of 0.0342 (\CI 0.0324--0.0362) and 0.0401 (0.0361--0.0440) (\cref{fig:sv-result}). The one-hop-minus-strict gain was 0.0058 (0.0033--0.0084). Conversely, adding $S$ after $C+T$ yielded $-0.0002$/$-0.0005$, with intervals spanning zero. These results concern the evaluated segment features and event representation; they do not show that sequence is generally uninformative for SVs.

Gains persisted across allele-frequency groups and adequately powered length strata. Approximately 27 examples per run in the 100-kb--1-Mb stratum produced uncertain negative estimates, which remain reported (\cref{tab:s-sv-length}). Complexity did not amplify the SV gain: strict estimates were 0.0419, 0.0380 and 0.0302 in low, medium and high groups, and one-hop estimates were 0.0499, 0.0474 and 0.0336 (\cref{fig:sv-complexity}). Donor stratification excluding five overlapping donors gave 60-donor macro gains of 0.0367/0.0430. The probes still used those donors' labels on other chromosomes, so this is not donor-held-out fitting (\cref{fig:s-donor}).

\subsection{Structural controls distinguish graph information from learned-weight gains}
\label{sec:controls-result}

Hidden-connection prediction was highly sensitive to simple structure. Preferential attachment reached strict AUPRC 0.9835 and degree sum 0.9742, exceeding original \method. Auditing candidate construction identified a masking-induced endpoint-degree deficit: positives lose visible connections while matched negatives need not. Unmasked degree/component inputs provide an additional route to infer missing structure. The one-hop reversal against these baselines does not establish removal of the shortcut (\cref{tab:s-controls}). A replay of 271,796 visible-graph baseline scores across 1,215 slices reproduced both contexts exactly.

The completed 120-run trained/random/$H$ downstream matrix provides a more direct test. After $C+S+H$, strict INS/DEL trained-minus-random AP was 0.005295 (\CI 0.002787--0.010515); one-hop was 0.000419 ($-0.002326$ to 0.005154). Strict cCRE favored random weights by 0.000759 (trained-minus-random interval $-0.001515$ to $-0.000123$), and one-hop was inconclusive (\cref{tab:s-hr}). No AP contrast passed the exact-test/BH correction. Thus the main graph gains do not establish a consistent learned-weight advantage across tasks. Full-graph $H$ also has broader context than windowed $T$.

Increasing unlabeled training windows improved reconstruction in all four tested fractions, but the completed 360-run biological scaling study was task and context dependent (\cref{fig:s-scaling}). A separate GraphMAE-style masked-feature candidate, $E$, uses frozen sequence inputs \citep{hou2022graphmae}. It exceeded matched random controls after $C+S+H$ on both development tasks at every seed, with mean gains 0.005609 INS/DEL and 0.001836 cCRE. These initializations share one validation chromosome block; no chromosome interval or completed replication result is available. The full development results and the failed junction candidate remain in \hyperref[sec:masked-development]{Supplementary Information}. This is evidence for further evaluation, not an established replacement for $T$.

\subsection{Cross-resource and external evaluations define the limits of reuse}
\label{sec:generalization-result}

Historical v1 reconstruction reached mean window-level AUPRC 0.9501/0.9880 on HPRC R2 and 0.9745/0.9868 on HGSVC3 under strict/one-hop contexts (\cref{fig:generalization-transfer,tab:s-reconstruction}). HPRC-to-HGSVC transfer reached 0.5997/0.9408; HGSVC-to-HPRC reached 0.7247/0.8902. R1.1-to-R2 transfer was 0.7063/0.7846, and R2-to-R1.1 was 0.5669/0.8838. These scores measure the original shortcut-sensitive objective. Graph construction, exposed context and shared donors prevent interpreting them as evidence of construction-invariant or donor-independent representations.

Prospective HG008 refits completed 30 evaluations on 69 clonal INS/DEL records without training on HG008. Gains were 0.032525 (\CI $-0.053371$ to 0.110254) strict and 0.064863 ($-0.066739$ to 0.212115) one-hop. Both are inconclusive, and six failed historical-probe replays remain separate from these refits. TraitGym's 14,780 matched variants yielded no positive topology interval in the locus-prior or allele-score analyses \citep{benegas2025traitgym}. Shallow boosting improved Mendelian $C+S+V$ AP from 0.102110 to 0.171056, but this is a classifier gain on previously inspected data, not improved encoder weights.

Measured COSIGT quality across 265 loci also showed no established topology benefit \citep{bolognini2026cosigt}. A training-median constant outperformed all fitted primary-target models; a validation-only fallback retained this negative result. These data predict released sample--locus quality and do not measure improvements to genotype calls. Full contrasts and limitations appear in \hyperref[sec:s-transfer-boundaries]{Supplementary Information}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\method frames human pangenome representation learning as a test of reusable information: encode native connectivity without biological labels, freeze the representations, and compare them with coordinate, sequence and structural controls on held-out chromosomes. Its methodological contribution lies in the oriented-segment formulation, coupled positional and graph processing, and controlled transfer design. Graph attention, gating and the optimization objective are established tools adapted to this setting. The present evidence supports complementarity for particular tasks, rather than universal superiority of a foundation model.

The strongest original gain occurs in classification of known insertions and deletions. Extending to three event types retains a macro increment after measured length and handcrafted graph controls, while adjusted inversion effects remain uncertain. This distinction matters: event-type prediction is not breakpoint discovery, genotyping or functional-effect prediction. The insertion pseudo-end convention and ascertained callset also limit mechanistic interpretation. Smaller gains in high-complexity SV regions contradict the proposed concentration of benefit there; the differing cCRE pattern cannot be generalized to SVs.

Regulatory results provide a complementary boundary. Sequence remains the larger independent source for cCRE classification, and most EN-TEx transfer tasks are null or inconclusive. Some allele-specific assays show modest pointwise increments, but their shared segment vectors describe locus susceptibility rather than donor-specific allelic activity. Exposure matching addresses measured opportunities to call AS without eliminating all ascertainment. These results motivate narrower biological hypotheses instead of selecting favorable assays to imply broad regulatory improvement.

Attribution is the central methodological limitation. A shortcut-sensitive reconstruction objective can reward readily available degree information, and random message passing captures much of the downstream graph gain. The completed controls identify a strict INS/DEL learned-weight increment with a positive pointwise interval but no consistent advantage across contexts and tasks. The separate masked-feature candidate addresses a different target and passes development controls; independent chromosome and external comparisons are still needed. Neither greater capacity nor improved intrinsic reconstruction alone establishes better biological reuse.

The evaluation also leaves representation limits. It withholds chromosomes rather than donors; shared donors and aggregate graph construction preclude unseen-individual claims. The evaluated nucleotide representation samples long segments at their ends, and stronger or variant-centered sequence baselines could alter conditional gains. Unconnected pairs are computational negatives, not verified haplotype-specific absences. The current SV graph lacks paths, so personal regulatory, methylation and genotype effects require verified path correspondence or a separately scoped model. QTL/GWAS regional-overlap analyses likewise do not establish enrichment, fine-mapping or causality.

A productive next step is to evaluate richer sequence--graph learning and explicit haplotype context under the same controls, with development-separated graph resources and biological outcomes. Cross-resource harmonization must distinguish population variation from graph-construction differences. The retained negative external and measured-quality results define where present representations have not demonstrated utility. Together, the method and its audits offer a reproducible basis for testing what population graph structure contributes, and what must still be learned before broader genomic reuse can be claimed.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Oriented-segment representation and coupled coordinate--graph encoder}
\label{sec:architecture}

The model operates on oriented segments and their native connections, rather than a linearized nucleotide input (\cref{fig:method}a,b). A segment $s$ has two handles, $2s+o$ for orientation $o\in\{0,1\}$. Reversing a handle toggles its orientation; the reverse-complement-equivalent relation to $(u,v)$ is $(v\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}1,u\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}1)$. The later canonical implementation uses this equivalence for candidate identity and masking. Historical training used exact directed rows, as detailed below; neither formulation makes the ordered pair scorer inherently reverse-complement equivariant.

Original v1 input $x_v$ has seven columns: genomic offset SO, segment length LN, rGFA SR annotation, GRCh38 membership, oriented degree, orientation and component index. SO, LN and degree are transformed by $\log(1+|x|)$ and divided by the slice maximum; SR and component index are min--max scaled, with constant columns set to zero. Membership and orientation remain binary. The structural columns are computed before query masking in original v1, a limitation addressed explicitly in the shortcut audit. No nucleotide embeddings or biological labels enter this encoder.

A shared input projection produces $h_v^{(0)}$. At layer $\ell$, coordinate attention $A_\ell$ and incoming-edge graph attention $G_\ell$ operate on the same evolving state. Thus ``coordinate'' specifies the attention neighborhood, not a branch restricted to coordinate-only inputs. With concatenation $\Vert$ and elementwise product $\odot$, the update is
\begin{align}
a_v^{(\ell)}&=A_\ell(h^{(\ell)},\mathrm{SO},o)_v, &
b_v^{(\ell)}&=G_\ell(h^{(\ell)},E_{\mathrm{visible}})_v,\\
g_v^{(\ell)}&=\sigma\!\left(W_\ell[a_v^{(\ell)}\Vert b_v^{(\ell)}]+c_\ell\right),\\
h_v^{(\ell+1)}&=\operatorname{LN}_\ell\!\left(h_v^{(\ell)}+g_v^{(\ell)}\odot a_v^{(\ell)}+(1-g_v^{(\ell)})\odot b_v^{(\ell)}\right).
\end{align}
The gate is learned per layer and feature; it is not only a final mixture of two independent embeddings. The principal backbone uses dimension 48, two layers and four heads. Graph attention follows the stored edge direction \citep{velickovic2018gat}; edge attributes were disabled in the principal configuration. Coordinate attention uses a window of SO-sorted node ranks, $k=\operatorname{clip}(\operatorname{int}[32(1+4b)],32,256)$, where $b$ is the slice branching fraction; this is not a base-pair radius. It combines three-scale rotary position encoding with orientation information \citep{su2021roformer}. The original sparse implementation normalizes attention over edges arriving at each destination and aggregates source values. We retain these original semantics when describing $T$; later bidirectional or chunked implementations are separate configurations. This combination adapts established attention and gating operations to native pangenome segments.

\subsection{Query masking, connection scoring and optimization}
\label{sec:objective}

The self-supervised target is observed connectivity within non-overlapping 5-Mb graph regions (\cref{fig:method}a,c). Positive candidates are observed adjacencies. Negative pairs are sampled within the same region, using the canonical benchmark's genomic-distance and unmasked-degree matching. They are not experimentally verified haplotype absences. Later canonical audits excluded conflicting reverse-complement-equivalent candidates; these post hoc exclusions do not retroactively change historical pretraining.

Historical v1 removed exact directed positive query rows for each candidate minibatch, together with candidate-level validation/test positives. Other training connections remained context. The later canonical implementation also removes reverse-complement-equivalent traversals. The historical audit found no duplicate reciprocal link rows in the released main graphs, but the old dense prediction IDs cannot prove absence of reverse-equivalent candidate leakage. We retain this uncertainty instead of assigning the later repair to the original checkpoints. Masking uses one encoder pass per query set, not per pair or per entire set of training edges. Original degree/component columns remain unmasked; positive and matched negative candidates can differ in visible-degree deficit. Distance matching therefore does not prevent the audited shortcut.

The ordered scorer consumes $[h_u\Vert h_v]$ through widths $2d\rightarrow2d\rightarrow d\rightarrow1$, layer normalization, ELU and dropout. Let $p=\sigma(s_{uv})$, $p_t=yp+(1-y)(1-p)$, $\alpha_t=0.25y+0.75(1-y)$, and $w_t=y\,n_-/\max(n_+,1)+(1-y)$ for candidate-batch counts $n_+,n_-$. The adopted binary focal objective \citep{lin2017focal} is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{edge}}=\operatorname{mean}_{(u,v)}[-\alpha_t w_t(1-p_t)^2\log p_t].
\end{equation}
Probabilities are clipped to $[10^{-7},1-10^{-7}]$. This pretraining scorer differs from the downstream SV pair features.

The principal launcher uses AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}, learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, dropout 0.1, DropEdge 0.1, 512 candidates per minibatch, four-batch gradient accumulation and gradient clipping at norm 1. Training is limited to 100 epochs with patience 20 and a validation macro-AUROC improvement tolerance of $10^{-4}$. The scheduler applies a five-step linear ramp followed by cosine decay and is stepped after each epoch: the first epoch uses the initial learning rate, followed by the recorded ramp. We state this ordering to reproduce the implemented schedule rather than implying a conventional warmup from the first update. These loss and optimizer choices are adopted techniques, not claimed methodological inventions.

\subsection{Frozen segment pooling and conditional information comparisons}
\label{sec:frozen-reuse}

After pretraining, eligible unmasked windows are encoded with fixed parameters (\cref{fig:method}d). The original segment cache averages embeddings over its oriented-handle occurrences across eligible windows. Biological examples join these vectors to the same interval/segment mapping used by their baseline features. Neither biological labels nor target-resource evaluation update the graph encoder or the independently pretrained Nucleotide Transformer.

Let $C$ denote the inherited coordinate/structural baseline, $K$ k-mer composition, $S$ frozen Nucleotide Transformer features and $T$ the original fused topology-pretrained features. Their definitions and joins are fixed across paired probes. We measure
\begin{align}
\Delta_{T\mid C,S}&=\operatorname{AUPRC}(C+S+T)-\operatorname{AUPRC}(C+S),\\
\Delta_{S\mid C,T}&=\operatorname{AUPRC}(C+S+T)-\operatorname{AUPRC}(C+T).
\end{align}
\label{sec:primary}
The first contrast tests additional graph-derived information, not a pretraining benefit by itself. The $H$ and matched random-encoder controls below test learned-weight attribution. $E$ denotes a separate sequence-conditioned development representation and is never substituted for historical $T$ results.

\subsection{Pangenome resources and graph preparation}


We analyzed graph resources derived from HPRC and HGSVC \citep{hprc2023draft,hgsvc2021,hgsvc32025}. We generated graph tables from GFA and GBZ resources using the variation graph toolchain \citep{garrison2018variation,siren2022gbz}. For each oriented segment, we recorded length, genomic coordinate when available, and connections to other oriented segments. HPRC-centered analyses used GRCh38-style chromosome names.

The processed HPRC R2 graph contains 751,237 segments and 1,097,658 links. The 303,425 downstream sequence-cache rows and 479,477 benchmark sequence-cache rows are distinct coverage denominators, not whole-graph segment counts. Full-coverage analyses used non-overlapping 5-Mb graph regions. Each candidate pair was represented by segment orientation, genomic separation, endpoint node degree, and observed connection status.

The separate path-resource audit identifies 227 HPRC donors and 454 phased haplotypes, and 61 HGSVC donors and 122 phased haplotypes (\cref{tab:s-path}). These counts describe the audited path-bearing resources, not a newly reconstructed donor provenance for the SV-resolution training graph. The fixed graph/checkpoint versions are unchanged. The SV graph itself has no donor paths; full-resolution path node IDs cannot be substituted for its segments without a verified correspondence.

\subsection{Strict and one-hop graph context}


We evaluate two graph contexts. In the strict setting, message passing is restricted to the native 5-Mb region after queried directed rows have been removed. In the one-hop setting, the encoder additionally receives segments directly connected to the region. The settings use the same genomic-region partition and archived run labels, but expansion can change the eligible candidate set. Their candidate counts are not assumed identical. Because one-hop expansion exposes more nodes and connections, strict and one-hop are treated as separate prediction contexts rather than exposure-matched estimates of a causal neighborhood-radius effect.

\subsection{Chromosome-held-out evaluation}

We partitioned the primary chromosomes into five rotating folds. Fold A tested chr1, chr6, chr11, chr16, and chr21; fold B tested chr2, chr7, chr12, chr17, and chr22; fold C tested chr3, chr8, chr13, chr18, and chrX; fold D tested chr4, chr9, chr14, chr19, and chrY; and fold E tested chr5, chr10, chr15, and chr20. The next fold in the rotation served as validation, with fold E validated on fold A; all remaining chromosomes were used for training. Original v1 used three independent executions labeled 42, 314159 and 20260806. These labels controlled split/order generators but did not fully seed PyTorch initialization or DropEdge in the historical trainer. They identify archived executions rather than exactly reproducible initialization seeds. Later campaigns use the repaired global seeding implementation; original checkpoint results are not retroactively relabeled as deterministic reruns.

Chromosome holdout prevents the same local genomic region from appearing in both training and evaluation. It is not a donor holdout. At least five donors (HG00733, HG02818, NA19036, NA19240, and HG002/NA24385) occur in both HPRC and HGSVC, so cross-resource analyses measure transfer between graph resources rather than donor-independent generalization.

\subsection{Reconstruction and cross-resource transfer}

We evaluated within-resource reconstruction on HPRC R2, HGSVC3, and integrated graph regimes. The primary metric was area under the precision--recall curve (\auprc), reported together with candidate prevalence. The distance-matched candidate sets are approximately balanced: HPRC R2 test prevalence is 0.4990 under strict context and 0.5065 under one-hop context; random-ranking AUPRC equals prevalence. For cross-resource and cross-version transfer, a trained model was applied to a different evaluation graph without target-resource adaptation. Strict and one-hop contexts were evaluated separately.

\subsection{Baselines, component controls, and capacity}

We evaluated four baselines on the same segment pairs as \method. The coordinate baseline used genomic coordinates, the k-mer composition baseline used nucleotide composition, the degree-sum baseline used the sum of endpoint node degrees, and the preferential-attachment baseline used their product. Degree sum and preferential attachment test whether a learned representation improves on simple connectivity statistics associated with the queried endpoints.

Degrees were computed on each context's actual visible graph, including the expanded one-hop graph, after removing the positive query edge and its reverse-complement counterpart. A replay audit reproduced 271,796 baseline scores over 1,215 context-specific slices exactly (maximum absolute difference zero).

Native 5-Mb complexity is an equally weighted mean of six robustly normalized measurements: log-transformed connection density, branching fraction, log maximum degree, log cycle-rank density, log segment density, and log segment-length interquartile range divided by median length. Except for branching fraction, transformations use $\log(1+x)$. Components are centered by their median and scaled by $1.4826$ times median absolute deviation; zero-scale components contribute zero, and standardized values are clipped to $[-5,5]$. The fixed score boundaries are $-0.155641$ and $0.434725$, giving 203 low-, 202 medium-, and 203 high-complexity windows. Thresholds were defined without model outcomes and reused across contexts. SV strata use the maximum endpoint category, with a start-only sensitivity; single-locus tasks use their mapped coordinate.

Component analyses removed the coordinate branch, graph branch, adaptive genomic window, fusion gate, or positional encoding. A separate capacity analysis compared hidden-dimension/graph-layer combinations of 24/1, 96/4, and 192/6 under the chromosome-held-out design.
The principal 48/2 model was recovered from the archived main matrix for a matched capacity comparison with identical fold/execution cells and held-out candidate counts. Its fold-pooled AUPRC is 0.9423; the headline HPRC value 0.9501 instead averages window-level AUPRC. Because AP is nonlinear, these two summaries are not interchangeable. Larger-capacity diagnostic results do not establish a monotonic scaling law.

\subsection{Sequence embeddings}

We generated sequence embeddings with the Nucleotide Transformer v2 50M multi-species model \citep{dalla2025nucleotide}. Model revision \texttt{81b29e5786726d891dbf929404ef20adca5b36f1} was used for all analyses, and its parameters remained frozen. The final data set contained 303,425 unique graph segments, each represented by a 512-dimensional sequence embedding, with complete coverage of the \ccre and SV tasks.

For sequences within the model input limit, we averaged the final hidden state over non-special, non-padding tokens (maximum 1,000 tokens). For longer segments, we sampled equal amounts of sequence from the beginning and end, up to 6,000 bases in total, with an N separator before applying the pinned tokenizer's maximum-length rule. This procedure affected 26.3\% of required segments. Four processing batches were merged after verifying a common embedding dimension, finite values, unique segment identifiers, a common archived model version, and complete task coverage.


\subsection{Cis-regulatory element classification}

We derived \ccre labels from the ENCODE registry and assigned them to HPRC graph segments \citep{encode2020registry}. Downstream classifiers were trained under the same rotating chromosome partitions using fixed combinations of coordinates, k-mer composition, sequence embeddings, and graph embeddings.
The original cCRE/INS--DEL probes used a training-fitted StandardScaler followed by balanced logistic regression (L2, $C=1$, lbfgs, maximum 800 iterations). The original architecture and regularization were fixed across those tasks; the completed extensions below disclose their separate 4,000-iteration budgets and probe sensitivities. Temperature calibration and the F1 operating threshold used validation chromosomes only. The mean per-run training size was 182,054 examples (range 164,434--201,721); validation and test each averaged 60,685 examples (50,846--73,988), rather than 60,685 combined.

\subsection{Structural-variant insertion/deletion classification}

We used HGSVC sequence-resolved INS/DEL records of at least 50 bp and mapped their GRCh38 endpoint coordinates to the existing HPRC R2 SV-resolution segments. The target is insertion ($y=1$) versus deletion ($y=0$), conditional on an existing variant record. It does not test breakpoint detection, inversion prediction, somatic status, pathogenicity or clinical effect. The mean training size was 104,343 examples (91,932--116,366); validation and test each averaged 34,781 (27,708--43,217).

For each information source, the ordered pair representation is $[x_{\rm left}\Vert x_{\rm right}\Vert|x_{\rm left}-x_{\rm right}|\Vert x_{\rm left}\odot x_{\rm right}]$. It is not invariant under swapping endpoints. When an insertion lacks an END field, the legacy normalizer infers a pseudo-end from variant/reference length; this is a representation convention rather than a second biological insertion breakpoint. We preserve that convention for manuscript comparability. Class-only results report insertion sensitivity or deletion specificity at the original validation-derived threshold; AP and AUROC are undefined within a single target class.

\subsection{EN-TEx biological transfer}

EN-TEx provides personal epigenomic measurements and supplied allele-specific (AS) calls \citep{rozowsky2023entex}. P0 aggregates 5,330,335 informative cCRE measurements into 250,722 testable loci: 28,092 positive (11.20\%) and 222,630 negative. A positive has at least one supplied significant call; a negative has accessible tested measurements and no significant call. Unmeasured elements are not negatives. Multiple overlapping graph segments are mean-pooled using the existing mapper; length-weighted pooling is supported. Assay, donor, tissue and measurement counts are retained. Before fitting, we specified exact 1:1 exposure matching by chromosome and informative-measurement count, plus separate H3K27ac-only and CTCF-only P0 tasks. This sensitivity controls measurement opportunities rather than read depth or all sources of ascertainment.

P1 uses explicitly active or repressed distal enhancer-like loci, excluding conflicting locus/tissue states. ENCODE V2 registry ENCFF924IMH matches all source accessions; later registry versions did not. Five tissues were selected before fitting by the larger available smaller-class count, requiring at least 1,000 loci per class: thyroid gland, tibial nerve, pancreas, gastroesophageal sphincter and Peyer's patch. Separate probes use identical chromosome folds; tissue macro estimates weight the five tissues equally within each fold/seed.

P2 uses the default accessible SNV call set, because the downloaded high-confidence file contains only RNA-seq. Chunked parsing of 22,310,439 rows retained CTCF (2,391,015 measurements, 596,650 loci; 3.51\% AS) and H3K27ac (3,168,525 measurements, 713,418 loci; 2.36\% AS). Each SNV uses its containing segment's frozen features. All occurrences of a genomic position remain on the same chromosome partition across donors and tissues. Measurement multiplicities are retained through count-equivalent probe fitting; all occurrences of a locus receive the same prediction. This tests locus-level susceptibility, not donor-specific imbalance direction. Read counts, p-values and supplied AS calls never enter input features. The completed RNA ASE and ATAC/H3K4me3/H3K27me3 extensions use the same chromosome-partitioned locus-feature framework. Their main paired comparisons and every predefined P0 sensitivity are reported in \cref{tab:s-entex}.

\subsection{Structural strata and donor analysis}

All strata reuse archived test predictions with identical feature universes. Native complexity, inherited size/frequency bins, carrier count and class were specified before the new subgroup scores. We report within-stratum AP, AUROC and prevalence-normalized AP, $(\mathrm{AP}-\pi)/(1-\pi)$, where $\pi$ is positive prevalence. This rescaling is not fully prevalence invariant. Strata require at least 100 examples and 20 of each class in every run for the adequately powered designation. Sparse and undefined outcomes remain in the tables.

Phased carrier assignments were joined by variant ID and coordinate from the original 65-donor VCF. Missing alleles remain unknown. Verified sample aliases identify HG002 and NA24385 as the same donor \citep{ncbi_hg002}; the five overlapping donors are marked separately. Donor and haplotype scores stratify the existing pooled, chromosome-held-out probes. They do not constitute donor-held-out fitting or personal graph embeddings. Because donors share variants, uncertainty is summarized over chromosome folds and seeds rather than independent donor resampling.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

All method comparisons were paired within biological task, chromosome fold, random seed, graph context, and information-source combination. We estimated confidence intervals with 10,000 hierarchical bootstrap replicates, resampling chromosome folds as the outer unit and random seeds within folds. This procedure preserves dependence among methods evaluated on the same held-out examples. Context interactions were defined as the one-hop contribution minus the strict-context contribution. Confidence intervals are reported for individual comparisons and are not adjusted for multiplicity across the full experiment suite. For historical SV subgroup analyses, exact two-sided sign flips use seed-averaged fold gains, and Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment is applied within each context, stratum family and metric. The completed SV-type studies adjust within each metric over all declared classes/macro summaries, contexts and contrasts. The H/random analysis likewise reports within-metric BH results. No chromosome confidence interval is assigned to the one-block masked-feature development study. Five nonzero fold differences give a minimum two-sided p-value of 0.0625; the pending four-fold primary summary has a minimum of 0.125. Positive bootstrap intervals do not override this limited exact-test resolution or establish BH-confirmed significance. Overlapping cross-validation training sets and correlated loci limit causal or population-level interpretation. Reconstruction intervals use 5,000 chromosome-block bootstrap draws retaining window counts; the earlier equally weighted chromosome-mean intervals were corrected without changing the point estimates. The reconstruction headline averages window-level AP, whereas the capacity diagnostic averages AP after pooling predictions within each fold; the statistics are labeled separately.

\subsection{Supplementary analyses and reproducibility}

We extracted haplotype paths from GBZ graph files and linked them to full-resolution segment indexes. Observed traversals were sampled within 5-Mb windows. We did not generate path-specific unobserved examples because absence from an aggregate graph and absence from a single haplotype require different definitions.

For external-signal overlap, we selected the highest-scoring 10\% of 5-Mb regions within each chromosome and matched each to five controls on region length, GC content, Umap/Bismap mappability \citep{karimzadeh2018umap}, graph complexity, HPRC variant density, and distance to a GENCODE v50 gene \citep{frankish2023gencode}. Signal sets included chr8 eQTL and sQTL positions \citep{lappalainen2013transcriptome}, all-trait GWAS Catalog positions, and Parkinson-filtered positions \citep{sollis2023gwas}.
The QTL analysis was a prespecified chromosome-8 pilot: only the chr8 HGSVC2 summary files were retrieved after checking download size and local storage; this restriction was not selected from enrichment outcomes.
We used 10,000 one-sided matched permutations and 2,000 bootstrap replicates for interval estimation.

For each principal analysis, we recorded model settings, trained-model version, held-out chromosomes, random seed, graph context, excluded conflicting pairs, predictions, and performance metrics. Summaries were generated after all fold-seed combinations completed. The final sequence--graph analysis contained 30 complete \ccre evaluations and 30 complete SV evaluations (15 fold-seed combinations per context). Exact software versions and file-integrity records are retained in the accompanying provenance package.

\subsection{Separating neighborhood information from simple connectivity cues}

In the original one-hop benchmark, the relative performance reversed. \subsection{Coverage of sequence embeddings}
\label{sec:s-coverage}

We generated 512-dimensional Nucleotide Transformer embeddings for 303,425 unique graph segments, covering every segment required for the \ccre and SV tasks. All four processing batches used the same archived model, and quality-control checks confirmed finite values and unique segment identifiers. For segments exceeding the model input limit, we sampled equal sequence from the beginning and end, up to 6,000 bases before applying the standard maximum-length rule. This procedure affected 26.3\% of required segments and motivates the scoped interpretation of the reciprocal sequence contribution in the main text.

\subsection{Graph complexity and donor-stratified SV effects}


The new complexity analysis reused all 30 archived probe runs. Strict-context topology gains were 0.0419 in low-, 0.0380 in medium-, and 0.0302 in high-complexity windows; one-hop gains were 0.0499, 0.0474 and 0.0336, respectively (\cref{fig:sv-complexity}). The high-minus-low interaction was $-0.0117$ (\CI $-0.0230$ to $-0.0019$) strict and $-0.0162$ ($-0.0267$ to $-0.0092$) one-hop. AUROC and prevalence-normalized AP showed the same ordering. Thus these data do not support the hypothesis that the representation helps more strongly in high-complexity SV regions. Exact five-fold sign-flip tests gave interaction $p=0.125$ and $p=0.0625$; the pointwise bootstrap intervals should not be read as multiplicity-adjusted mechanistic evidence.

\subsection{Controlled training-window scaling}

We retained the same 52,033-parameter encoder and connection scorer, exact HPRC R2 graph, objective, five chromosome folds and three seeds. Training windows were selected by a deterministic chromosome-balanced ranking at requested fractions of 12.5\%, 25\%, 50\% and 100\%; smaller sets are nested. Rounding within each chromosome and inherited unusable-slice exclusions mean realized fractions differ slightly from these requested values. Validation and test windows were unchanged, and the saved validation/test candidate identities and labels match exactly across fractions within every fold, seed and context. All 120 runs completed with the same 100-epoch limit and patience of 20. Parameter shapes, checkpoint metadata and source hashes were audited. The 100\% comparator was newly trained with the same current deterministic seeding and canonical-conflict exclusion, rather than substituted from historical results.

Pooled held-out-fold AP increased from 0.9184 at 12.5\% to 0.9292, 0.9375 and 0.9420 at 25\%, 50\% and 100\% under strict context. One-hop values were 0.9635, 0.9721, 0.9847 and 0.9900. The paired 100\%-minus-12.5\% gains were 0.02360 (95\% CI 0.02155--0.02575) and 0.02646 (0.02529--0.02792), respectively. Intervals use the paired fold/seed bootstrap. These scores pool candidates within each held-out fold and therefore differ from the window-mean reconstruction headline. Mean completed epochs ranged from 77.5 to 99.7 across context/fraction groups; training time and optimizer updates were not held constant. This establishes a reconstruction learning curve under the stated training protocol, not a scaling law, a diversity-scaling result, or demonstrated improvement in biological transfer.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/intrinsic_scaling.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Held-out reconstruction improves with more unlabeled training windows.} Left, mean pooled held-out-fold AUPRC across five chromosome folds and three seeds. Right, paired differences from the newly trained 100\% control on identical test candidates. Whiskers are 95\% hierarchical fold/seed bootstrap intervals. Lines connect measured settings without fitting a scaling law. Axes use logarithmic spacing for the requested fractions. All models have the same parameter count, but effective training compute varies with the number of windows and early stopping.}
\label{fig:s-scaling}

The complete 120-run window-scaling experiment shows increasing held-out reconstruction as more training windows are available (\cref{fig:s-scaling}). This is a data-and-compute learning curve: the architecture and epoch limit are fixed, but optimizer updates are not held constant. The completed 360-run frozen biological evaluation is task dependent: the 100\%-minus-12.5\% C+S+T AP change was positive under strict SV transfer (+0.00348, 95\% CI 0.00309--0.00385) but negative under one-hop SV transfer (-0.00151, 95\% CI -0.00283-- -0.00066); cCRE improved in one-hop context (+0.00090, 0.00037--0.00146) but was inconclusive in strict context (-0.00032, -0.00066--0.00003), and CTCF intervals included zero. Thus intrinsic reconstruction scaling does not imply universal biological transfer.
